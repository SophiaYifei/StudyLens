# Computer Vision I — Foundations of CNNs and Modern Practice

## 1. Introduction to Computer Vision

### 1.1 Core Tasks in Computer Vision

Computer vision (CV) concerns automated understanding of images and videos. Major task categories include:

- **Image Classification**: Assign a single label to an image (e.g., dog vs. cat).
- **Object Detection**: Detect and localize objects using **bounding boxes**.
- **Image Segmentation**:
  - **Semantic segmentation**: Assign a class to each pixel.
  - **Instance segmentation**: Separate individual object instances.
  - **Object masking**: Identify object regions precisely.
- **Keypoint Detection**: Detect landmark points (e.g., joints in pose estimation).
- **Object Tracking / Motion Analysis**
- **Scene Reconstruction** (3D modeling, AR/VR)

### 1.2 Application Areas

- **Autonomous driving**
- **Medical imaging** (region-of-interest detection)
- **Manufacturing & agriculture**
- **Surveillance & sports analytics**
- **Facial recognition & product identification**

---

## 2. Challenges in Computer Vision

Real-world image data is complex and noisy:

- **Occlusion** (objects partially hidden)
- **Lighting variation**
- **Background clutter**
- **Pose & perspective changes**
- **Scale variation**
- **Ambiguity in depth/spatial reasoning**

Additional constraints:

- Need for **very large labeled datasets**
- Requirement for **fast inference** (e.g., real-time systems)
- Deployment mismatch between curated training data and messy real-world data

---

## 3. Image Representation

An image is a tensor:

- Dimensions: **H × W × C**
  - Example: `1080 × 1920 × 3`
- Each pixel contains **three channels (RGB)**:
  - Example: `[214, 197, 205]`

Thus, images are large high-dimensional arrays.

---

# 4. Traditional Computer Vision

Before deep learning, CV relied on **manual feature engineering** followed by classical ML models.

## 4.1 Handcrafted Feature Types

### 4.1.1 Color-Based Features

- **Color Histograms**
- **Color Moments** (mean, standard deviation, skewness)
- **Dominant Colors** (via clustering, e.g., k-means)
- **Color Coherence Vectors** (spatial awareness of color regions)

---

### 4.1.2 Texture-Based Features

Capture spatial relationships of pixel intensities:

- **GLCM (Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix)**
  - Derived metrics: contrast, correlation, entropy
- **Local Binary Patterns (LBP)**
- **Gabor Filters**
- **Haralick Features**

Applications: fingerprint recognition, MRI/CT analysis, surface inspection.

---

### 4.1.3 Statistical Features

- Mean, variance, standard deviation
- **Entropy** (image complexity)
- **Zernike Moments** (rotation-invariant descriptors)
- **Wavelet Transforms** (multi-scale analysis)

---

### 4.1.4 Shape Features

- **Edge Detection**: Sobel, Canny, Prewitt
- **Contour Descriptors**
- **Hu Moments** (invariant to rotation, scale, translation)
- **SIFT / SURF** (keypoint detection, transformation robustness)
- **HOG (Histogram of Oriented Gradients)**

---

## 4.2 Classical ML Models

Used after feature extraction:

- **SVM** (high-dimensional classification)
- **Random Forest**
- **Gradient Boosting / XGBoost**
- **k-NN**
- **Decision Trees**

---

## 4.3 Limitations of Traditional CV

- Requires **domain expertise**
- Loses spatial relationships
- Cannot model **hierarchical patterns**
- Poor generalization
- High-dimensional feature vectors
- Expensive at high resolutions

This motivates **deep learning**.

---

# 5. Neural Networks for Images

## 5.1 Fully Connected Networks Are Inefficient

Example:

Input: `228 × 228 × 3`

Flattened size:
\[
228 \times 228 \times 3 = 155,952
\]

If connected to 10 neurons:
\[
155,952 \times 10 + 10 = 1,559,530 \text{ parameters}
\]

This is inefficient and ignores spatial locality.

---

# 6. Convolution

## 6.1 Intuition

Convolution is a **sliding window operation**:

- A **kernel/filter** slides across input.
- At each position, compute a **weighted sum**.

---

## 6.2 1D Convolution

For time-series:
\[
y[i] = \sum_k x[i+k] \cdot w[k]
\]

Used for signals.

---

## 6.3 2D Convolution (Images)

Kernel slides across width and height:

\[
\text{Output}(i,j) = \sum_{m,n} X(i+m,j+n) \cdot W(m,n)
\]

---

## 6.4 3D Convolution

For videos or volumetric data:

- Convolution across width, height, and depth/time.

---

# 7. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)

## 7.1 Key Ideas

- **Local connectivity** (receptive fields)
- **Weight sharing**
- **Translation invariance**
- Dramatic reduction in parameters

Example:

Filter size: `3 × 3 × 3`, 10 filters:

\[
3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 10 + 10 = 280 \text{ parameters}
\]

vs. 1.5 million in fully connected layer.

---

## 7.2 CNN Components

### 7.2.1 Convolutional Layer

- Filters of size \( F \times F \times D \)
- Output: **Feature maps**
- Each filter learns specific patterns

---

### 7.2.2 Stride (S)

- Number of pixels the filter moves per step.
- Larger stride → downsampling.

---

### 7.2.3 Padding (P)

Adds zeros around borders.

Output size formula:

\[
\text{Output} = \frac{W - F + 2P}{S} + 1
\]

“Same” convolution:
\[
P = \frac{F - 1}{2}
\]

---

### 7.2.4 Pooling Layer

Downsampling operation:

- **Max pooling** (most common)
- **Average pooling**

Typically:

- Filter: `2 × 2`
- Stride: `2`

Benefits:

- Reduces computation
- Provides spatial invariance
- Acts as regularization

---

## 7.3 Canonical CNN Pipeline

\[
\text{Conv} \rightarrow \text{BatchNorm} \rightarrow \text{ReLU} \rightarrow \text{Pooling}
\]

Repeat multiple times.

Then:

\[
\text{Flatten} \rightarrow \text{Fully Connected} \rightarrow \text{Softmax}
\]

---

# 8. CNN Architectures

## 8.1 LeNet (1990s)

- First successful CNN.
- Used for digit recognition.

---

## 8.2 AlexNet (2012)

- ImageNet breakthrough.
- Much deeper than LeNet.
- Popularized CNNs.

---

## 8.3 VGGNet (2014)

- Stacked **3×3 convolutions**
- Deep but homogeneous architecture.
- Showed small filters outperform large ones.

---

## 8.4 Accuracy Saturation Problem

Deeper plain networks:

- Training error increases
- Optimization becomes difficult
- Not overfitting — optimization failure

---

## 8.5 ResNet

Introduced **skip connections**:

\[
y = F(x) + x
\]

Benefits:

- Enables identity mapping
- Improves gradient flow
- Solves vanishing gradient
- Enables very deep networks (50, 101, 152 layers)

---

## 8.6 Inception

Parallel convolutions:

- 1×1
- 3×3
- 5×5
- Max pooling

Concatenate outputs.

Advantages:

- Multi-scale feature extraction
- Parameter efficiency
- State-of-the-art in 2014

---

# 9. Transfer Learning

Most modern CV uses pretrained models.

## 9.1 Feature Extraction

- Use pretrained backbone (e.g., ResNet on ImageNet)
- Remove final layer
- Add custom classification head
- Freeze backbone weights

---

## 9.2 Fine-Tuning

- Start with pretrained weights
- Unfreeze some/all layers
- Use smaller learning rate
- Train for fewer epochs

---

# 10. Data Augmentation

Improves robustness.

Apply **only to training data**.

Common transforms:

- Random cropping
- Rotation
- Horizontal flipping
- Brightness/saturation jitter
- Scaling

In:

- **PyTorch**: `transforms` + `ImageFolder`
- **TensorFlow**: `ImageDataGenerator`

Each epoch sees new augmented samples.

---

# 11. Implementation Notes

## 11.1 Output Size Example

Given:

- \( W = 28 \)
- \( F = 3 \)
- \( P = 0 \)
- \( S = 1 \)

\[
\frac{28 - 3 + 2(0)}{1} + 1 = 26
\]

Pooling:

\[
\frac{26 - 2}{2} + 1 = 13
\]

Flatten example:

\[
5 \times 5 \times 20 = 500
\]

---

## 11.2 Practical Workflow

1. Choose pretrained backbone (ResNet/VGG/Inception)
2. Apply transfer learning
3. Add thoughtful data augmentation
4. Evaluate generalization
5. Iterate architecture if needed

---

# Summary of Key Concepts

- Traditional CV relied on **manual feature engineering**.
- CNNs use **local receptive fields + shared weights**.
- Convolution reduces parameters dramatically.
- Pooling enables spatial invariance.
- Deep networks require architectural innovations (ResNet).
- Modern practice relies heavily on **transfer learning**.
- Data augmentation improves robustness to real-world variation.
- Architecture design balances **accuracy vs. computational cost**.
