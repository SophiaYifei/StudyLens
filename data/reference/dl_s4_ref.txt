## 1. Overview of Natural Language Processing (NLP)

### 1.1 Definition
**Natural Language Processing (NLP)** is the field of artificial intelligence concerned with enabling machines to understand, represent, generate, and reason over human language.

Unlike images (which are naturally represented as pixel intensity matrices), text must be transformed into numerical representations before being processed by machine learning models.

## 3. Challenges in NLP

### 3.1 Linguistic Challenges

- **Polysemy (Homonymy)**  
  One word, multiple meanings  
  - *“bank”* (river bank vs. financial bank)

- **Synonymy**  
  Multiple words, same meaning  
  - *sneakers / tennis shoes / running shoes*

- **Context Dependency**
  Observations are not independent.
  - “The dog ate the bone. It tasted good.”  
    → What does “it” refer to?

- **Semantic Ambiguity**
  - “I saw the boy on the beach with my binoculars.”

- **Slang and Colloquialisms**
  - “That jump was sick.”

- **Acronyms**

- **Variable-Length Sequences**
  - Sentences and documents vary in length.

- **Sarcasm and Humor**
  - Difficult for literal models to interpret.

## 4. Text Representation

### 4.1 One-Hot Encoding

- Vocabulary size = \( V \)
- Each word → vector of length \( V \)
- Exactly one position = 1, others = 0

Example:
"cat" → [0,0,1,0,...,0]


**Problems:**
- Extremely high-dimensional and sparse
- No semantic similarity captured
- “cat” as different from “kitten” as from “nuclear”

---

## 5. Traditional NLP Methods

### 5.1 Bag of Words (BoW)

- Represents document as word frequency vector
- Ignores grammar and word order
- Vocabulary = all unique words in corpus

Example:
"the cat sat" → {the:1, cat:1, sat:1}


**Limitations:**
- Order ignored
- Sparse representation
- Overemphasizes common words

---

### 5.2 TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency)

Improves upon BoW by down-weighting common words.

#### Term Frequency (TF)
\[
TF(t,d) = \frac{\text{count of } t \text{ in } d}{\text{total words in } d}
\]

#### Inverse Document Frequency (IDF)
\[
IDF(t) = \log\left(\frac{N}{df_t}\right)
\]

- \( N \) = total documents
- \( df_t \) = documents containing term \( t \)

#### TF-IDF Score
\[
TF\text{-}IDF(t,d) = TF(t,d) \times IDF(t)
\]

**Intuition:**
- Common words → low IDF → low weight
- Rare but document-specific words → high weight

---

### 5.3 N-grams

Capture local word order.

- **Unigram**: 1 word
- **Bigram**: 2 consecutive words
- **Trigram**: 3 consecutive words

Example:
- "machine learning is cool"
- Bigrams: [machine learning], [learning is], [is cool]


Advantages:
- Captures local context
- Distinguishes “not good” from “good”

Limitations:
- Vocabulary explosion
- Increased sparsity

---

### 5.4 Cosine Similarity for Retrieval

Used in classical document retrieval.

\[
\text{cosine similarity} = \frac{A \cdot B}{||A|| \, ||B||}
\]

- 1 → identical
- 0 → orthogonal

Used in TF-IDF-based retrieval systems (precursor to neural retrieval).

## 6. Text Preprocessing Pipeline

### Recommended Order

1. **Lowercasing**
2. **Punctuation removal**
3. **Tokenization**
4. **Stopword removal**
5. **Stemming or Lemmatization**

---

### 6.1 Tokenization

Splitting text into units.

#### Word-level
"I love NLP" → ["I", "love", "NLP"]
- Simple
- Struggles with out-of-vocabulary (OOV)

#### Subword (e.g., BPE)
"unhappiness" → ["un", "happy", "ness"]

- Handles OOV
- Used in GPT and BERT

#### Character-level
["I"," ","l","o","v","e"]

- Robust
- Long sequences, limited semantics

---

### 6.2 Stopwords

Common words with low semantic content:
- the, is, a, of, and, etc.

**Caution:**  
Removing stopwords may alter meaning:
"not good" → remove "not" → "good"


---

### 6.3 Stemming vs. Lemmatization

| Method | Description | Output Quality | Speed |
|--------|------------|---------------|--------|
| **Stemming** | Rule-based suffix removal | May produce non-words | Fast |
| **Lemmatization** | Dictionary-based normalization | Real words | Slower |

Examples:
- running → run
- studies → study
- better → good (lemmatization only)

---

## 7. Word Embeddings

### Motivation
One-hot vectors do not encode meaning.

Goal:
- Map words into **dense, low-dimensional vectors**
- Similar words → similar vectors

---

### 7.1 Word2Vec (2013, Mikolov et al.)

Shallow neural network with one hidden layer.

#### Two Architectures

**1. CBOW (Continuous Bag of Words)**
- Context → predict center word
- Faster
- Better for frequent words

**2. Skip-gram**
- Center word → predict context
- Better for rare words

---

### Word2Vec Properties

- Dense embeddings (100–300 dimensions typical)
- Captures semantic relationships
- Enables vector arithmetic:

\[
king - man + woman \approx queen
\]

- **Static embeddings**  
  Same vector regardless of context.

Limitation:
- “bank” has identical embedding in all contexts.

---

### 7.2 GloVe (2014)

- Uses global co-occurrence matrix
- Matrix factorization approach
- Combines global and local statistics
- Static embeddings

---

### 7.3 FastText (2016)

- Extends Word2Vec
- Uses character n-grams
- Handles OOV words
- Effective for morphologically rich languages
- Static embeddings

---

## 8. Sequence Models

Traditional models ignore order. Sequence models capture dependencies.

---

### 8.1 Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)

Hidden state update:

\[
h_t = \tanh(W_x x_t + W_h h_{t-1} + b)
\]

- Processes tokens sequentially
- Suffers from **vanishing gradient problem**

---

### 8.2 LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory)

Introduces:
- **Cell state**
- **Forget gate**
- **Input gate**
- **Output gate**

Additive memory updates allow gradients to flow over long sequences.

Advantages:
- Handles long-term dependencies better than vanilla RNN.

Limitations:
- Sequential computation (not parallelizable)
- Slow training

---

### 8.3 GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit)

- Simplified LSTM
- Two gates:
  - Update gate
  - Reset gate
- No separate cell state

Often:
- Comparable performance to LSTM
- Fewer parameters

---

## 9. Limitations of RNN-Based Models

- Slow (sequential processing)
- Difficult hyperparameter tuning
- Struggle with very long sequences
- Cannot fully capture long-range dependencies

These limitations motivate **Transformers**, which use self-attention (covered next week).

## 10. Implementation Considerations

### 10.1 Handling Variable-Length Sequences

Neural networks require fixed-size tensors.

#### Padding
- Add `<PAD>` tokens
- Use padding masks to ignore during training

#### Packed Sequences (PyTorch)
- Avoid unnecessary computation on padded tokens

---

### 10.2 Data Augmentation for Text

Techniques:
- Back-translation
- Synonym replacement
- Random insertion/deletion
- Random word swapping

Challenges:
- Small changes can alter meaning
- Must preserve label integrity

---

### 10.3 Handling Imbalanced Data

Approaches:
- Oversampling minority class
- Undersampling majority class
- Class-weighted cross-entropy loss
- SMOTE (with embeddings)
- Two-stage training
- Ensemble methods

---

### 10.4 Data Splitting Best Practices

Avoid data leakage.

- Chronological splits for temporal data
- Split conversation threads as units
- Avoid duplicate documents across splits
- Maintain domain consistency

---

## 11. Transfer Learning and Fine-Tuning

Strategies:

- **Full Fine-Tuning**
  - Update all parameters

- **Frozen Backbone**
  - Train only task-specific layers

- **Partial Freezing**
  - Freeze lower layers

- **Gradual Unfreezing**
  - Unfreeze layers progressively during training

## 14. Key Definitions

- **Polysemy**: One word, multiple meanings  
- **Synonymy**: Multiple words, same meaning  
- **Tokenization**: Splitting text into units  
- **Stemming**: Rule-based word root extraction  
- **Lemmatization**: Dictionary-based normalization  
- **TF-IDF**: Frequency × inverse document rarity  
- **Static Embedding**: Same vector in all contexts  
- **Contextual Embedding**: Vector depends on surrounding words
