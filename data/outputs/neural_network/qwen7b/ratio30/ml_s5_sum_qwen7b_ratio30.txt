### Summary of Lecture on Classification Models

#### Introduction to Classification
- **Definition**: Predict a categorical target (e.g., handwritten digits, tumor type, disease category).
- **Types**:
  - Binary (two classes): Benign vs. malignant tumors, Type of lung disease.
  - Multiclass (multiple classes): Income in bins, Sales lead scoring.
- **Key Differences from Regression**:
  - Cannot extrapolate beyond training data.
  - Cannot predict values outside the range of observed data.

#### Logistic Regression
- **Objective**: Predict the probability \(P(y=1)\) instead of \(y\).
- **Function**: Use the **sigmoid (logistic)** function to ensure outputs are between 0 and 1.
- **Mathematical Formulation**:
  \[
  P(y=1) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(w_0 + w_1x)}}
  \]
- **Parameter Estimation**:
  - **Cost Function**: Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) instead of Sum of Squared Errors (SSE).
  - **Optimization**: Gradient Descent to minimize Negative Log Likelihood (NLL).

#### Decision Boundary and Threshold
- **Threshold**: Default is 0.5; adjust to improve precision and recall.
- **Effect of Threshold**: Higher threshold increases precision but decreases recall, and vice versa.

#### Logistic Regression in scikit-learn
- **Methods**:
  - `model.predict`: Returns class labels.
  - `model.predict_proba`: Returns probabilities.
- **Regularization**: L2 penalty (ridge) by default.
- **Features**: Still a linear model, but the decision boundary is linear.

#### Softmax Regression for Multiclass Classification
- **Objective**: Predict the probability of each class.
- **Function**: Apply **softmax** to the scores obtained from the weighted sum of features.
- **Mathematical Formulation**:
  \[
  P(y=k) = \frac{e^{z_k}}{\sum_{j} e^{z_j}}
  \]
- **Cost Function**: Cross Entropy, equivalent to MLE for binary classification.

#### K Nearest Neighbors (KNN)
- **Approach**: Assign class based on the majority vote of the k nearest neighbors.
- **Parameters**:
  - **k**: Number of neighbors.
  - **Distance Metric**: Euclidean, Cosine Similarity.
  - **Feature Scaling**: Important to prevent dominance of features with larger scales.
- **Trade-offs**:
  - Small k: Low bias, High variance (risk of overfitting).
  - Large k: High bias, Smooth decision boundary.

#### Support Vector Machines (SVM)
- **Objective**: Find the optimal separating hyperplane.
- **Core Concepts**:
  - **Margin**: Maximizing the margin between classes.
  - **Support Vectors**: Points closest to the margin.
- **Soft Margin SVC**:
  - Allows some misclassification to achieve a wider margin.
  - Hyperparameter \(C\) controls the trade-off between margin width and errors.
- **Kernels**:
  - **Linear Kernel**: Simplest form.
  - **Polynomial Kernel**: Adds polynomial interactions.
  - **Radial Basis Function (RBF) Kernel**: Uses distances from landmarks.
- **Implementation in scikit-learn**:
  - Standardize features first.
  - Choose appropriate kernel and \(C\); tune via cross-validation.
- **Pros and Cons**:
  - Advantages: Flexible with kernels, works well in high dimensions, convex optimization.
  - Disadvantages: Slow for large datasets, often outperformed by tree ensembles or neural networks, no native probabilistic output.

#### Concept Review Questions
- **Difference between Logistic Regression and Softmax**: Logistic regression is for binary classification, while softmax extends to multiclass classification.
- **Why Cross Entropy Better for Classification**: Cross entropy penalizes incorrect classifications more heavily than MSE, leading to better performance.
- **Bias-Variance Tradeoff in KNN**: Smaller k reduces bias but increases variance, and vice versa.
- **Role of C in SVM**: Controls the trade-off between margin width and errors; larger C leads to a narrower margin and fewer training errors.

### Detailed Summary

#### Introduction to SVM
- **Purpose**: SVM is an algorithm used for both regression and classification tasks.
- **Popularity**: Historically, SVM has been widely used, particularly for classification problems.
- **Non-linearity**: Enables efficient learning of non-linear decision boundaries.

#### History and Development
- **Early Methods**: Before 1980, most statistical learning methods focused on linear decision boundaries.
- **Advancements**: From the 1980s onwards, decision trees and neural networks improved efficiency in handling non-linear relationships.
- **Modern Techniques**: In the 1990s, SVM developed advanced methods for non-linear functions with strong theoretical foundations.

#### Mathematical Formulation
- **Objective**: Find the hyperplane that maximizes the margin between classes.
- **Margin Definition**: Distance between the hyperplane and the closest data points (support vectors).
- **Support Vectors**: Data points lying on the margin.

#### Margin and Support Vectors
- **Margin Calculation**: Perpendicular distance between the hyperplane and the closest data points.
- **Support Vectors**: Points defining the margin.
- **Mathematical Relationship**: Margin \(\gamma = \frac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}\)

#### Effect of Hyperparameter C
- **Trade-off**: Balances between maximizing the margin and minimizing classification error.
- **Impact**: 
  - Larger \(C\): Tighter fit to training data, prioritizes reducing classification error.
  - Smaller \(C\): Looser fit, focuses on maximizing the margin.

#### Types of Kernels
- **Linear Kernel**: Basic form without non-linear transformations.
- **Polynomial Kernel**: Adds polynomial interactions between features.
- **Radial Basis Function (RBF) Kernel**: Transforms data into a higher-dimensional space using distances from landmarks.

#### Implementation in scikit-learn
- **Steps**:
  - **Data Scaling**: Ensure proper scaling of features.
  - **Instantiation**: Create an SVM object.
  - **Kernel Selection**: Choose between linear, polynomial, or RBF.
  - **Hyperparameter Tuning**: Set \(C\) to balance margin and misclassification.
  - **Cross-Validation**: Optimize \(C\) and kernel parameters using cross-validation.

#### Feature Scaling
- **Importance**: Necessary for SVM to function correctly.
- **Process**: Normalize or standardize features to ensure equal contribution.

#### Role of the Decision Boundary
- **Definition**: Hyperplane that separates classes.
- **Influence**: Position influenced by support vectors.

#### Advantages and Disadvantages
- **Advantages**:
  - Flexibility through kernel choices and regularization.
  - Effective in high-dimensional spaces.
- **Disadvantages**:
  - Often outperformed by modern models like random forests or neural networks.
  - Computationally intensive for large datasets.
  - Lack of inherent probabilistic output, requiring post-processing.
