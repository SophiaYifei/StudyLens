### Key Differences Between Stratified Sampling and Weighted Sampling

#### Stratified Sampling
- **Definition**: Divides the population into homogeneous subgroups (strata) based on characteristics such as age, income, or region, and then samples from each stratum proportionally.
- **Purpose**: Ensures that each subgroup is adequately represented in the sample, reducing sampling bias.
- **Implementation**: Uses stratified random sampling to ensure that the sample reflects the population's diversity.
- **Implications**:
  - **Bias Reduction**: Reduces sampling bias by ensuring that each subgroup is represented.
  - **Model Performance**: Can lead to more reliable and generalized model performance because the sample accurately mirrors the population.
  - **Complexity**: Requires prior knowledge of the population structure and can be computationally intensive.

#### Weighted Sampling
- **Definition**: Assigns weights to each sample unit based on its importance or representativeness. These weights are used to adjust the influence of each unit in the analysis.
- **Purpose**: Adjusts the sample to reflect known population characteristics or to give more weight to certain units.
- **Implementation**: Uses weighting schemes to adjust the sample composition.
- **Implications**:
  - **Bias Reduction**: Can correct for known biases in the sampling process.
  - **Model Performance**: Improves model performance by giving more weight to units that are underrepresented in the sample.
  - **Flexibility**: Allows for more flexibility in adjusting the sample composition without needing to define strata explicitly.
