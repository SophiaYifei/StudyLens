#### **1. Definition and Characteristics of Time Series:**

- **Definition:** 
  - A time series is a sequence of data points observed at successive equally spaced points in time.
  - It has a natural temporal ordering.
  
- **Difference from Time-Stamped Data:**
  - Time-ordered data isn't necessarily a time series; e.g., medical records.
  - Sensors and hardware data commonly represent time series.

- **Real-World Examples:**
  - Temperature
  - Power demand
  - Retail/bookstore sales

#### **2. Trend and Seasonality:**

- **Components:**
  - **Trend:** Long-term movement (increasing/decreasing).
  - **Seasonality:** Cyclical patterns (hourly, daily, weekly, yearly).

- **Simultaneous Existence:**
  - Both can coexist in a single time series.

- **Examples:**
  - Temperature
  - Power demand
  - Retail sales

#### **3. Time Series Modeling:**

- **Assumptions:**
  - Standard regression assumes independence among observations.
  - Time series violate this assumption (present is correlated with past).

- **Key Concept: Autocorrelation:**
  - Correlation of a signal with a delayed (lagged) copy of itself.
  - Measured using Pearson correlation or Durbin-Watson test.

- **Methods:**
  - ARIMA (AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average)
  - Exponential Smoothing

- **Question:**

#### **4. Time Series Decomposition:**

- **Components:**
  - **Trend:** Long-term movement.
  - **Seasonality:** Cyclical patterns.
  - **Residual:** Error or leftover component.

- **Models:**
  - **Additive Model:**
    \[
    Y = \text{Trend} + \text{Seasonality} + \text{Residual}
    \]
    Linear trend, constant seasonal amplitude.
    
  - **Multiplicative Model:**
    \[
    Y = (\text{Trend}) \times (\text{Seasonality}) \times \text{Residual}
    \]
    Non-linear trend, seasonal amplitude grows with trend.

- **Question:**
  - If seasonal swings get larger as values increase, which model fits better?

- **To-Do:**
  - Look at a dataset and decide between additive or multiplicative seasonal decomposition.

- **Function:**
  - `statsmodels`' `seasonal_decompose()` method.
  - Requires estimating the period.
  - Must choose additive or multiplicative.
  - Automated detection may overfit patterns.

- **Validation:**
  - Apparent seasonality may be noise.
  - Validate by checking if the residuals are small compared to the seasonal component.

- **Question:**
  - How would you validate that seasonality is real?

- **To-Do:**

#### **5. Markov Processes:**

- **Concept:**
  - Full autocorrelation at lag 1.
  - Current state depends only on the previous state.
  - Transition matrix defines probabilities.

- **Applications:**
  - Used in science, finance, and ranking algorithms (e.g., PageRank).

- **Question:**

Introduction to Stationarity in Time Series:
Stationarity refers to a time series whose statistical properties, such as mean and variance, remain constant over time. Stationary time series are crucial for accurate modeling and forecasting. Non-stationary time series, on the other hand, exhibit trends, seasonality, or structural changes, making them challenging to model accurately. Transformations such as differencing or logarithmic transformations can be applied to make a non-stationary time series stationary. An example of a stationary time series is a random walk with a constant mean and variance. Stationarity ensures that the model parameters remain stable, leading to reliable forecasts. Non-stationary time series can lead to spurious regression results and unreliable parameter estimates. Handling non-stationary time series involves identifying and removing trends and seasonality, often through differencing or other transformations. Stationarity is a fundamental concept in time series analysis, impacting the choice of models and the accuracy of forecasts. Understanding stationarity is critical for effective time series modeling.
