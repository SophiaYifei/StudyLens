### Summary of University Lecture on Time Series Analysis

**Overview:**
The lecture covers the foundational concepts of time series analysis, focusing on understanding the unique characteristics and modeling techniques applicable to time-ordered data. Key topics include defining time series, distinguishing them from time-stamped data, and exploring the components of time series like trend and seasonality.

---

#### **1. Definition and Characteristics of Time Series:**

- **Definition:** 
  - A time series is a sequence of data points observed at successive equally spaced points in time.
  - It has a natural temporal ordering.
  
- **Difference from Time-Stamped Data:**
  - Time-ordered data isn't necessarily a time series; e.g., medical records.
  - Sensors and hardware data commonly represent time series.

- **Real-World Examples:**
  - Temperature
  - Power demand
  - Retail/bookstore sales

- **Questions:**

  - Identify 3 real-world time series examples and 1 dataset that is time-ordered but not a time series.

#### **2. Trend and Seasonality:**

- **Components:**
  - **Trend:** Long-term movement (increasing/decreasing).
  - **Seasonality:** Cyclical patterns (hourly, daily, weekly, yearly).

- **Simultaneous Existence:**
  - Both can coexist in a single time series.

- **Examples:**
  - Temperature
  - Power demand
  - Retail sales

- **Questions:**

#### **3. Time Series Modeling:**

- **Assumptions:**
  - Standard regression assumes independence among observations.
  - Time series violate this assumption (present is correlated with past).

- **Key Concept: Autocorrelation:**
  - Correlation of a signal with a delayed (lagged) copy of itself.
  - Measured using Pearson correlation or Durbin-Watson test.

- **Methods:**
  - ARIMA (AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average)
  - Exponential Smoothing

#### **4. Time Series Decomposition:**
