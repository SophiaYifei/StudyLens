     \[

   - **Optimizers**: SGD, Momentum, Adagrad, RMSprop, Adam.
   - **Regularization Techniques**: Early stopping, learning rate schedules, L1/L2 regularization, Batch Normalization, Dropout.

# Elaboration on Activation Functions

## Role of Activation Functions

Activation functions play a crucial role in neural networks by enabling non-linearity and controlling the output range. They act as decision-makers, deciding how much of the signal should be passed forward in the network. Common activation functions include:

- **Sigmoid**: Maps \( z \rightarrow (0, 1) \). Useful for binary classification tasks.
  \[
  \sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}
  \]

- **ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit)**: Outputs zero for negative inputs and a linear relationship for positive inputs. Efficient and easy to use.
  \[
  \phi(z) = \max(0, z)
  \]

- **Softmax**: Maps \( z \rightarrow (0, 1) \) for each class, ensuring the sum across all classes equals one. Used in multi-class classification tasks.
  \[
  \text{softmax}(z_i) = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}
  \]

- **Leaky ReLU**: Similar to ReLU but allows a small positive slope for negative values, mitigating the "dying ReLU" problem.
  \[
  \phi(z) = \max(a \cdot z, z)
  \]
  where \( a \) is a small constant (e.g., 0.01).

### Impact on Training Process and Model Performance

- **Non-Linearity**: Activation functions introduce non-linearity, allowing neural networks to learn complex patterns.
- **Control Output Range**: They control the output range, making the model easier to understand and interpret.
- **Smoothness and Convergence**: Non-linear activation functions can make the error surface non-convex, affecting the ease and speed of training.

## Examples of When to Use Different Types of Activation Functions

- **Binary Classification**: Use Sigmoid or Softmax for output layer.
- **Regression Tasks**: Use ReLU or Leaky ReLU for hidden layers.
- **Multi-Class Classification**: Use Softmax for output layer.
- **Feature Extraction**: Use ReLU for hidden layers to preserve non-linearity.

# Detailed Explanation of the Training Loop

## Steps Involved

1. **Initialize Weights**: Randomly initialize the weights \( W \) and biases \( b \).
2. **Forward Propagation**: Propagate the input \( x \) through the network layers to generate an output \( \hat{y} \).
3. **Calculate Loss**: Measure the discrepancy between the predicted output \( \hat{y} \) and the actual label \( y \) using a loss function \( L \).
4. **Backpropagation**: Compute the gradients of the loss with respect to the weights and biases.
5. **Update Weights**: Adjust the weights and biases using gradient descent to minimize the loss.

### Relevant Formulas

- **Forward Propagation**:
  \[
  z_l = W_l \cdot a_{l-1} + b_l
  \]
  \[
  a_l = \phi(z_l)
  \]
  where \( z_l \) is the pre-activation, \( a_l \) is the activation, \( W_l \) is the weight matrix, \( b_l \) is the bias vector, and \( \phi \) is the activation function.

- **Loss Calculation**:
  \[
  L(y, \hat{y}) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n l(y_i, \hat{y}_i)
  \]
  where \( l \) is the element-wise loss function.

- **Backpropagation**:
  \[
  \delta_l = \phi'(z_l) \odot \delta_{l+1} \cdot W_{l+1}^T
  \]
  \[
  \Delta W_l = \alpha \cdot \delta_l \cdot a_{l-1}^T
  \]
  \[
  \Delta b_l = \alpha \cdot \delta_l
  \]
  where \( \phi' \) is the derivative of the activation function, \( \odot \) denotes element-wise multiplication, \( \alpha \) is the learning rate, and \( \Delta W \) and \( \Delta b \) are the updates to the weights and biases.

### Diagrams

```plaintext
Forward Propagation:
x -> W1 -> a1 -> W2 -> a2 -> ... -> Wn -> an = y_hat

Loss Calculation:
y -> y_hat -> L(y, y_hat)

Backpropagation:
an -> Wn -> δn -> W(n-1)^T -> δ(n-1) -> ... -> W1 -> δ1
```

# Types of Regularizations and Their Impact

## Regularizations

Regularizations are techniques used to prevent overfitting by adding constraints to the model. Common types include:

- **L1 Regularization (Lasso)**: Adds a penalty proportional to the absolute value of the weights.
  \[
  \lambda \sum_{i} |w_i|
  \]

- **L2 Regularization (Ridge)**: Adds a penalty proportional to the square of the weights.
  \[
  \lambda \sum_{i} w_i^2
  \]

- **Dropout**: Randomly drops neurons during training to promote diversity in the network.
  \[
  P(\text{drop neuron}) = p
  \]

- **Batch Normalization**: Standardizes the inputs of each layer to stabilize and accelerate training.
  \[
  \mu = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^m x_i
  \]
  \[
  \sigma^2 = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^m (x_i - \mu)^2
  \]
  \[
  x_{norm} = \frac{x - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}
  \]

### Diagrams

```plaintext
L1 Regularization:
λ * ||W||_1

L2 Regularization:
λ * ||W||_2^2

Dropout:
P(dropping neuron) = p

Batch Normalization:
μ = 1/m * ∑ x_i
σ^2 = 1/m * ∑ (x_i - μ)^2
x_norm = (x - μ) / sqrt(σ^2 + ε)
```

### Practical Tips

- **L1 Regularization**: Useful for feature selection, encouraging sparsity.
- **L2 Regularization**: Prevents overfitting by penalizing large weights.
- **Dropout**: Improves generalization by reducing co-adaptation of neurons.
- **Batch Normalization**: Accelerates training and improves stability.

By incorporating these regularizations, you can effectively prevent overfitting and enhance the overall performance of your neural networks.
