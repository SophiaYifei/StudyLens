### Summary of University Lecture Material on Time Series Analysis

#### Overview
The lecture covers the basics of time series analysis, focusing on understanding the unique characteristics and challenges of time series data compared to traditional regression datasets. Key topics include:

1. **Definition and Characteristics**:
   - **Time Series**: Sequence of data points equally spaced in time with a natural temporal order.
   - **Real-world Examples**: Temperature, power demand, retail sales.
   - **Difference Between Time Series and Time-Ordered Data**: Time-ordered data lacks the inherent temporal dependency.

2. **Components of Time Series**:
   - **Trend**: Long-term movement (increasing or decreasing).
   - **Seasonality**: Cyclical patterns repeating at fixed intervals (hourly, daily, weekly, yearly).

3. **Modeling Time Series**:
   - **Standard Regression Assumptions Broken**: Time series data violates the independence assumption.
   - **Autocorrelation**: Correlation of a signal with a delayed (lagged) copy of itself.
   - **Markov Process**: Current state depends only on the previous state; used in various fields like finance and ranking algorithms.

4. **Time Series Decomposition**:
   - **Components**: Trend, Seasonality, Residuals.
   - **Models**:
     - **Additive Model**: \( Y = \text{Trend} + \text{Seasonality} + \text{Residual} \)
     - **Multiplicative Model**: \( Y = (\text{Trend}) \times (\text{Seasonality}) \times \text{Residual} \)

5. **Validation Techniques**:
   - **Autocorrelation Plot**: Visualizes correlation with lagged versions of the data.
   - **Durbin-Watson Test**: Statistical test indicating the presence of seasonality.
   - **Markov Property**: Full autocorrelation at lag 1, current state depends only on previous state.

6. **Autoregressive Models**:
   - **Using Past Values as Features**: Lag features (past temperatures, weather conditions).
   - **Multiple Lags**: Including multiple past values to capture more complex patterns.

7. **Project 1**:

### Technical Details
- **Autocorrelation**: Measured using Pearson correlation or Durbin-Watson test.
- **Markov Process**: Full autocorrelation at lag 1, current state depends only on previous state.
- **Additive vs. Multiplicative Models**: 
  - Additive: Linear trend, constant seasonal amplitude.
  - Multiplicative: Non-linear trend, seasonal amplitude grows with trend.
- **Seasonal Decomposition**: Using `statsmodels`' `seasonal_decompose()` function.
- **Autoregression Model**: Using past values as features, including multiple lags.
